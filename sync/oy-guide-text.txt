“Cholam” vowel: exceptions
While the rule regarding the “stressed cholam in an open syllable” holds true in most cases, there are some exceptions:
[More common] Words with a stressed cholam in a closed syllable that nevertheless follow the Group 2 dialectal pattern. These items typically belong to a younger stratum of the Semitic component, i.e. borrowed more recently or ad-hoc into Yiddish, especially in the learned register. Examples: הוד, הון, המון, חובֿ, חול, לכבֿוד.
[Rare] Words with a stressed cholam in an open syllable that are nevertheless  pronounced “o” in all dialects. These words typically belong to the more ancient stratum the Semitic component. Example: יוסף.

“Cholam” vowel
Includes:
1. Virtually all other words spelled with ױ, so YOU DON’T NEED TO MEMORIZE THIS SET.  These primarily derive from ou or ō in MHG, but contain a smaller number of borrowings from Slavic. Vowel changes (e.g. in diminutive and plural) have ײ rather than  ײַ.
2. All words from the Semitic component that contain a stressed cholam in an open syllable (open = not capped by a consonant).  Thus, the plurals דורות and סודות fall into this category, while their singulars דור and סוד do not (they have “o” universally). See exceptions below.
Group 4: (Near-)universal “oy”
Group 3: NEY “oy” from hiatus repair
“Cholam” vowel + r (in NEY)
אױער is often pronounced as ever or eyver, and טױער as tever – though ey is common too.
Past participles with ־ױרן are typically pronounced (and often written, too) as ־אָרן instead: געבױרן becomes געבאָרן, etc. Same goes for פֿאַרלױרן,  געשױרן,  געשװױרן, and געפֿרױרן. However, pronunciation with ey exists as well.
Borrowings
Words where source language either has “oy” – or “ou”/”au” that was nativized* into Yiddish as “oy”.
• To facilitate pronouncing the vowe sequence /o+e/, NEY frequently inserts /y/: הנאה /hanoye/, גאָון /goyen/, טעות /toyes/, נאָענט /noyent/, בלאָע /bloye/, גראָע /groye/, etc.
• In the last two cases, the “oy” has even been generalized to the base form and explicitly written with ױ, giving בלױ and גרױ.
• For the non-Semitic words, SEY often repairs the hiatus by inserting /v/: נאָװנט /nuvnt/, גראָװ /gruv/, etc. In parts of both NEY and SEY, טעות features something similar: toves or tuves, respectively.

Appendix: Minimal (and Near-Minimal) Pairs
The vowel spelled with ױ in Yiddish is pronounced, in Standard Yiddish, as “oy”. However, in reality, ױ represents two distinct vowels whose pronunciation differs by dialect. Those wishing to speak with a particular dialect can use this guide to learn which is which. Critically, this is the only major phonetic detail that sets apart Standard Yiddish vowels from NEY (Northeastern, or “Litvish” Yiddish) Yiddish; CY (Central, or “Polish”) and SEY (Southeastern, or “Ukrainian”) further involve vowel length, covered here.
* However, if modern Hungarian Hasidic use ױ to spell modern English words with the “ou” sound, the intended pronunciation is not “oy”; the ױ is natural due to the “ou” sound ױ makes in words from Group 1.
** Speakers from regions in the German cultural sphere, including Hungarian/Unterland, may pronounce German borrowings such as these with /ou/.
